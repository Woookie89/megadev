## The five pillars

00:02:59,380 --> 00:04:50,000 [John Lindquist]

This week we'll focus on the five pillars of agentic tooling. Pillar one, composable. You keep one conversation at the center and add, swap, and grow agents around it, piece by piece, into the workspace optimized for your product. The critic you just saw is just one simple example. Pillar two, intuitive. You build so many agents and tools across so many projects, there's no way you can remember what they all do. So each agent surfaces its own examples, hints, autocomplete for you and your agents just in time. This way you never feel lost because everything is intuitive. In fact, anyone could jump into your project and start contributing. Pillar three, proven. Agentic tooling is built against evals. You write down what you expect, then use a big, smart model to force a small, fast model to fit those expectations. Simply put, your expectations are the ceiling. The more thought and effort you put into what you expect, the more your agents can align with you. Pillar four, glanceable. Agentic tooling is known for producing huge walls of indecipherable text. But you don't have to live that way. Agents can show a grade, a status, a comparison, examples. They can even write a song for you, although I don't recommend it. But putting work into making the output glanceable helps you stay informed without having to ask what's going on. And pillar five, homegrown. Your factory is already buried in your own product. You find it naturally as you uncover and fill in the gaps. So it fits you and your team. It is yours. Or, as I like to put it, the real software factory is the agents we made along the way. You grow agents in your personal lab to solve your problems, then they evolve to help your team, and then finally your customers.
